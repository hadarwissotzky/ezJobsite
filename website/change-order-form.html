What a change order should include

  • A number and a date, so each change can be referenced on the final invoice.
  • The scope in plain words—what is added, removed or swapped, and where. Attach a photo when you can.
  • The price, broken out: labor, materials, equipment, and any credit for work no longer needed.
  • Schedule impact, even when it is zero. "No change to schedule" settles the question up front.
  • The new contract total, so nobody is surprised at the last draw.
  • Signatures before the work starts. That is the part a verbal yes never gives you.

Why paper change orders get skipped

The client asks for "one more outlet while you're here," you are holding a drill, and the form is in the truck. So the work gets done on a verbal yes and argued about at the final invoice. The form above is fine for the office. On the jobsite, the faster path is the one in your pocket.

Change order questions

Does a change order need to be signed before the work starts?

It should be. A signature before the work starts is what turns a verbal yes into an agreement both sides can point to later. Check your contract and your state's rules for anything specific.

Can a client sign a change order by text?

Yes. With EZChangeOrders the client gets a link by text, reviews the scope, photos and price, and signs on their phone without installing anything.

Can I write the change order in Spanish?

In the app, yes—speak in Spanish and your client reads it in English.
